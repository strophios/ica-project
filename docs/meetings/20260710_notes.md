- On further refining ICA – this has been something we have been working with on the hand-coded historical articles that Min Jee and Audrey are reviewing. We have this (draft, detailed) coding document: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1hv-c-Do6mtmcTg5xrnlWsr7yb7w6UKGWM8TFFx1Zaeo/edit?tab=t.0. We can talk through this and your questions tomorrow, but if you have time and inclination, take a look tomorrow morning. Some quick points:
    - We have been thinking about ICA as CA + IMM + CLAIM, separating out the actor from the evidence of ‘concern to immigrants’ in the claim.
    - We also have three levels for CA and IMM, with “allies” = 1 for “immigrant,” reserving IMM=2 for relatively clear evidence of immigrant action. This is a bit different from our DoCA coding.
    - We are also debating, for the allies, whether the allyship is only relevant for documentation, access and (perhaps) labor claims. That is to say, if there is “diasporic” action, we don’t count “ally” action (e.g., a group of non-Jews doing collective action against pogroms in Russia). 
    - Further, we code “diasporic” as when there is a call on the US, not (merely) on a foreign gov’t to do something.
    - We are coding labor action, when immigrants are explicitly involved (or targets). We are not coding labor unions as “allies” unless they are speaking on behalf of imm workers.
